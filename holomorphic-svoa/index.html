Overview

For every positive integer \(k\) there is a holomorphic \(\mathcal N=1\) superconformal vertex operator algebra (a holomorphic SCFT, or holomorphic SVOA) of central charge \(c=12k\) whose Witten index is \(\pm24/\gcd(24,k)\), the TMF-predicted minimal nonzero absolute index: if the Segal–Stolz–Teichner picture holds, the index of every holomorphic SCFT with \(c=12k\) is divisible by \(24/\gcd(24,k)\) (index 0 is not excluded), and Gaiotto and Johnson-Freyd [GJF] asked whether this value is always attained. At \(k=24\) the minimum is 1: we have an explicit holomorphic SCFT \(T''\) with \(c=288\), a unique Neveu–Schwarz vacuum and index \(-1\) (index \(+1\) after stacking with the Arf theory), the candidate field theory for the periodicity class \(\Delta^{24}\in\pi_{576}\mathrm{TMF}\). All theories are built in the same way: a self-dual ternary code gives a lattice SCFT (GJF), a symmetry of the code is gauged, and the results are combined by symmetric squares and tensor products. The construction of \(T''\) from one code of length 72, the transfer-matrix trick and the \(D_4\) picture are explained in the paper; this page does not repeat them.

What this page adds is the material behind the paper's tables: an explicit code (generator matrix and symmetry) for every \(k\) that needs one, in a uniform machine-readable format with a stand-alone verifier; the argument that the quaternion orbifolds used at \(k=8\) and \(k=16\) are free of anomalies, the new theoretical input behind the finite-case orbifold constructions; the field content of \(T''\) at low weight, and the full characters of the seed theories of all 8,748 \(k=6\) codes (at least 4,579 inequivalent ones); the Landau–Ginzburg material behind the paper's Claim 1, a gapping of the doubled seed and the refined elliptic-genus identity with its four-spin-structure check (which concern the doubled seed, not the gapping of \(\overline{T''}\otimes T''\)); a proof of GJF's Conjecture 1.4 on self-dual ternary codes (24 divides the index at every positive length divisible by 12, and index 24 occurs at every such length; standalone version: ../gj-conjecture/); and a list of what is a theorem, what is an exact computation, what is quoted from the literature and what is still open. A companion page, details.html, gives the full arguments with a status label on every statement; it is written to be checked line by line, by people or by other agents.

What is proved, what is computed, what is cited

The labels below are used throughout details.html.

  • computed Every code fact. Self-duality, the code index \(\pm24\), the symmetry (that it preserves the code, its order and cycle type) and the balance of its fixed words are exact finite computations over \(\mathbb F_3\). Each certificate was checked by both teams with separately written code, and once more by the stand-alone verify.py on this page. Exceptions: the two \(k=16\) codes have been checked by the Claude team and by verify.py, not by Codex; Codex's \(k=7\) code by Codex and by verify.py.
  • cited From codes to theories. That a self-dual ternary code gives a holomorphic SCFT whose index is the code index, that monomial code automorphisms act on it preserving the supercurrent, and that a cyclic symmetry of the right kind can be gauged to divide the index by its order (Theorem 5.6), are theorems of GJF. We use them as published.
  • proved, modulo cited theorems The quaternion orbifolds at \(k=8,16\). GJF's theorem is about cyclic groups. On that route \(k=8,16\) need a cyclic code symmetry \(g\) with \(g^4=-1\): shift symmetries of chains of cells are excluded (proved), and all sampled codes with such a \(g\) satisfy the stronger divisibility of Conjecture A (see below). The quaternion group \(Q_8\) has the two properties GJF's index argument needs, so its orbifold has index \(24/8=3\) if the \(Q_8\) action has no 't Hooft anomaly. Part of that anomaly is invisible to every torus partition function, so it cannot be checked numerically. We prove it vanishes: at \(k=8\) the code is joined to 24 copies of the tetracode by 23 \(Q_8\)-equivariant neighbour steps (an exact computation; at \(k=16\) a chain to 48 copies exists by the general neighbour-chain argument and was not computed step by step); each step is a gauging that preserves the anomaly (a “gauging lemma”, proved here from the algebra-object description of twisted modules); at the end the theory is 192 (resp. 384) free fermions, whose anomaly is 24 (resp. 48) times that of eight fermions, and the anomaly group \(\mathrm{Hom}(\Omega^{\rm Spin}_3(BQ_8),U(1))\cong\mathbb Z_8\oplus\mathbb Z_4^2\) has exponent 8. The argument relies on published theorems (GJF, Kirillov, Creutzig–Kanade–McRae, McRae, Carnahan–Miyamoto, Davighi–Gripaios–Lohitsiri and others); it was reviewed in three rounds by the Codex team, all of whose points were addressed, but it has not been refereed. Details §F.
  • cited Symmetric squares and the \(D_4\) orbifold. The exchange symmetry of \(T\otimes T\) has anomaly \(2c\bmod 8\) (Albert–Kaidi–Lin), which vanishes at \(c=72,144\); the index formulas are elementary. Unique vacua follow from the positive ground energies of all twisted sectors.
  • computed Field content of \(T\), \(T'\) and \(T''\) (next section), in the standard orbifold construction of GJF: the lift of the symmetry (proved for the momentum states, and for the Ramond ground states from GJF's treatment of odd-order elements) and the modular-covariant action on twisted sectors (details §H.1).
  • character level, conditional open in equivariant tmf Compatibility with TMF. The topological counterpart of the symmetric square (the power operation \(P_2\), a String Thom isomorphism and the transfer over commuting pairs) reproduces the index arithmetic \(-4\Delta^6\mapsto2\Delta^{12}\mapsto-\Delta^{24}\) in Ganter's equivariant Tate K-theory at the level of characters (\(q\)-expansions), under the stated assumptions on the Thom normalisation and on the second spin lift (details §I.1a–I.1b); an integral construction, as a transfer in genuine \(\Sigma_2\)-equivariant tmf, remains open. The numerical chain itself is checked independently. Moreover \(\pi_{144},\pi_{288},\pi_{576}\,\mathrm{tmf}\) are torsion-free (Bruner–Rognes), so these classes are determined by their \(q\)-expansions. Details §I.
  • proved 24 divides the index of every self-dual ternary code whose length is a positive multiple of 12, the first half of GJF's Conjecture 1.4 (GJF proved it for lengths divisible by 24, and 12 | index otherwise). The new part is the factor 8, by an elementary argument: two self-dual codes meeting in codimension one have indices adding up to four times the index of their intersection, which is even, and every code is joined to a sum of tetracodes, of index 0, by such steps. Checked numerically, by an adversarial referee (a separate AI agent with its own code) and in the Codex team's audit, which found no gap; not yet refereed externally or reviewed by DP. Details §A.7; standalone version, cited by the paper: ../gj-conjecture/, Section 2.
  • proved Every length \(12k\), \(k\ge1\), has a self-dual ternary code of index 24, the second half of GJF's Conjecture 1.4; with the first half, the whole conjecture. The code is an explicit chain of \(k\) Golay codes coupled through two fixed vectors at each junction; its index \((-1)^k\,24\) follows from one exact identity \(Tu=-u\) for an \(81\times81\) transfer matrix (changing a coordinate sign if needed gives \(+24\)). Checked by the two programs used when it was found, by an independent recomputation, by a stand-alone script and in the Codex team's audit; the finite identity is also published as a sparse certificate. Not yet refereed externally or reviewed by DP. No higher-order symmetry is established for these codes, but they already give minimal-index theories for two infinite families: for \(\gcd(k,24)=1\) the code theory itself has index 24, and for \(\gcd(k,24)=2\) gauging the scalar \(-1\) (GJF Thm 5.6) gives index 12. The other \(k\) still need the constructions above. Details §A.8; standalone version, cited by the paper: ../gj-conjecture/, Section 3.
  • physics The link to TMF itself rests on the Segal–Stolz–Teichner conjecture; the minimal nonzero index is a target, not a theorem about all SCFTs. TMF motivates the target; its divisibility was checked after the exact computations.
Why not a cyclic group at \(k=8,16,24\)? On the route of GJF's Theorem 5.6 (an index-24 code with a cyclic code symmetry of order \(\gcd(24,k)\)), these \(k\) need a code symmetry \(g\) with \(g^4=-1\). For chains of cells whose symmetry is a shift, a 2-adic theorem (\(2^{v_2(L)+2}\mid\operatorname{Tr}JM^L\)) excludes it: shifts of order 8 or 24 never work (proved, details §C.3–C.4). For arbitrary signed code automorphisms with \(g^4=-1\) we prove that the index is congruent to 8 times the number of “mixed” orbits of full-support codewords modulo 16, and in about 72,000 sampled codes with such a symmetry (70,447 of length 96 by Codex, 1,450 of lengths 16–48 by Claude) every index is divisible by 16, in fact by 48 at lengths divisible by 24. That such codes always have nonzero absolute index at least 48 is Conjecture A (details §F.1.2), supported by these samples, not proved. This motivates the quaternions: a group of order 8 is needed on this quotient route, not for every conceivable construction of an index-3 theory. Details §F.1.

The index-1 theory, field by field

The paper (§4.3, “States and fields”) states the main facts: none of \(T\), \(T'\), \(T''\) has fields of weight \(\tfrac12\) or 1, each has 113, 2,928 and 42,001 fields of weight \(\tfrac32\), 2 and \(\tfrac52\) (the supercurrent \(G\) among the first), the NS character of \(T\) is a polynomial of degree 6 in \(t=(\vartheta_3/\eta)^{12}\), and the Ramond ground states are far from unique.   • Where the fields come from. Below weight 3 every field of \(T''\) is a symmetrised, \(\mathbb Z_6^4\)-invariant lattice vertex operator of 288 chiral bosons on four copies of \(\Lambda(C)\). The 113 fields of weight \(\tfrac32\) are the \(\mathbb Z_6\)-orbit sums of the 144 frame vectors \(\pm\sqrt3e_i\) (24 orbits, among them the supercurrent \(G\)) and of the 534 minimal vectors, one per weight-9 codeword (89 orbits). The first twist fields appear at weight 3: 16 + 16 from the \(g^{\pm2}\)-twisted sectors of \(T\). The swap-twisted sectors of \(T'\) and \(T''\) start at weights 6 and 12.
  • Ramond partition function. Substituting \(t\mapsto-u\), \(u=(\vartheta_2/\eta)^{12}=4096\,q\prod_n(1+q^n)^{24}\) (the Ramond character of 24 free fermions), in the paper's polynomial for \(Z^{\rm NS}_T\) gives the Ramond partition function, with all coefficients positive: \[Z^{\rm R}_T=13940+629867\,u+751404\,u^2+129679\,u^3+6984\,u^4+144\,u^5+u^6 ,\] and similarly for \(T'\) (degree 12) and \(T''\) (degree 24, constant term \(4.7\cdot10^{15}\)). Bosonic and fermionic parts are \(\tfrac12(Z^{\rm R}\pm\mathcal I)\).
  • What is assumed. The twisted sectors are computed from two standard inputs that are not re-derived: GJF's lift of the symmetry and the modular-covariant action on twisted sectors. Consistency checks that a wrong phase would most likely break (integrality, spin–statistics, level matching) all pass. Details §H.
  • Open. \(T''\) has index \(-1\) and about \(4.7\cdot10^{15}\) Ramond ground states. Whether some index-1 theory at \(c=288\) has a unique Ramond ground state is not known.

How the search worked, and what it cost

The paper explains the basic device: a code built as a closed chain of small “cells” has an index equal to the trace of a product of small transfer matrices, so testing one candidate (one random choice of the cells in a chain) costs milliseconds instead of \(2^{6k}\) sign evaluations. Measured on one Apple-silicon core (Python 3.12, numpy 2.5, exact integer arithmetic; code/time_candidates.py, notes/timings.json): building the transfer matrix of one width-12 cell takes 10 ms with 3 boundary spins (27×27) and 23 ms with 4 (81×81), once per cell of a pool; evaluating one candidate then takes 1.3 ms for a \(k=13\) chain of seven cells with 3 boundary spins, and 32 ms for a \(k=21\) period of four cells with 4 boundary spins (cubed, both closures, exact). The \(k=18,21\) workers first screen candidates modulo two primes; in the earlier benchmark that brought the \(k=21\) lane to about 3 ms per candidate, including pool building. The search sampled cell tuples at random: keep those whose trace is \(\pm24\) (and whose balance trace vanishes when a symmetry of order 3 or 6 is needed), and verify every hit from scratch on the expanded generator matrix. Codes are rare, between one in \(10^2\) candidates at \(k=6\) and one in \(10^6\)–\(10^7\) at \(k=18,21\). Three choices mattered more than raw speed:

  • Chains of different cells instead of one repeated cell: at \(k=14\) identical cells never got below \(|\text{index}|=144\), while \(2\cdot10^6\) random candidates gave 17 codes.
  • Four boundary spins (81-state transfer matrices) instead of three at \(k=18,21\): \(3\cdot10^8\) three-spin candidates by both teams never reached 24; the slower four-spin families did, after about \(10^7\) candidates at \(k=18\) and \(2\)–\(4\cdot10^6\) at \(k=21\).
  • A “second discriminant” for the space of boundary spins (an idea from Codex's random cells), which must be chosen for the whole chain, not cell by cell; in one-minute probes it gave two to three times as many small nonzero indices.

Things found along the way

  • The family of Hadamard–Paley cells behind the paper's seed is finite: in the exhaustive census with two to five boundary spins, 204,490 cells, of which exactly 298 inequivalent ones give \(k=6\) seeds, with a selection rule for the boundary spins (inequivalent under the cell symmetries, not a classification up to code equivalence or SCFT isomorphism; an incomplete six-spin phase adds 132 seeds in 60 more classes). §C Together with a separate census of reflection cells (7,112 seed codes) and 1,636 benchmark hits there are 8,748 distinct \(k=6\) codes (the paper's Table 1). Their complete characters (polynomials in \(t\), all published) distinguish at least 4,579 inequivalent seed theories and as many double symmetric squares; the low-weight NS spectra alone (the terms of the NS character from \(q^{-3}\) through \(q^{-1/2}\)), which need only the untwisted sector, distinguish 4,563. For the census subset the counts are 2,961 and 2,958. §G.3
  • A Landau–Ginzburg description of the doubled seed (the paper's Claim 1): \((\sum_i\Phi_i^3)/\!\!/C\) flows to \(\overline V_{\Lambda(C)}\otimes V_{\Lambda(C)}\), given the standard LG/minimal-model and orbifold inputs. Proved here: its Witten index \(\frac1{|C|}\sum_{c,c'}(-2)^{\#\{\text{common zeros}\}}=\mathrm{Index}(C)^2\), the refined identity \(Z_{\mathrm{LG}/C}=\mathrm{Index}(C)\,\chi^{\rm R}\) (quintuple product), and the equality of all four spin-structure partition functions of the IR orbifold and the double; a real superpotential gaps the double of one seed. It does not gap \(\overline{T''}\otimes T''\), whose symmetries act on one chirality. §L
  • A 2-adic theorem: for every anti-periodic chain of cells (any even widths, common boundary space), \(2^{v_2(L)+2}\) divides the index of \(L\) periods, \(L\ge4\). It rules out shift symmetries of order 8 and 24. §C.3
  • The simplest seed's \(9\times9\) transfer matrix can be derived by hand from seven integers fixed by an \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\) symmetry. §D
  • Within the family of permutation orbifolds we searched (all subgroups of \(S_n\), \(n\le6\), and \(S_N\), \(\mathbb Z_N\) for \(N=8,12,24\), with the spin twists of details §E.4: the seed Arf sign, the Kitaev twists and the discrete torsion restricted from the Pin cover), the chain \(4\to2\to1\) cannot be shortened. This is a statement within this family, not an optimality theorem. An extended search (all subgroup classes of \(S_N\) for \(N\le8\) with all twists, \(S_N\) and \(\mathbb Z_N\) up to \(N=23\), closure \(k\le23\)) finds only the step \(k=6\to12\); it uses floating-point screening with exact fallback, so it is not a certified exclusion, and degrees 9–11 are still running. The paper (§5) summarises both as a search of “all relevant choices of subgroups of \(S_N\)”. §E
  • Conjecture A (index divisible by 16 whenever \(g^4=-1\)) and a reduction of it to a parity statement. §F.1
  • A proof of GJF's Conjecture 1.4: 24 divides the index of every self-dual ternary code whose length is divisible by 12 (GJF had it for lengths divisible by 24), §A.7; and every length \(12k\) has a code of index 24, an explicit chain of Golay codes, §A.8. Both are also written up as a standalone note, ../gj-conjecture/.

How to verify

Everything a reader needs is in this folder. With Python 3 and numpy:

python3 code/verify.py --selftest      # transfer DP versus brute force on random small chains
python3 code/verify.py                 # all certificates, about half a minute

For each certificate the verifier rebuilds the code from its generator alone, checks self-duality over \(\mathbb F_3\), computes the index and the number of Ramond ground states exactly by a banded transfer computation that uses nothing but the generator, checks that the stated symmetry preserves the code and satisfies the hypotheses of GJF's Theorem 5.6 (or the quaternion relations), computes the balance of the fixed codewords by contracting the fixed subcode, and prints the orbifold index next to the TMF-predicted minimum \(24/\gcd(24,k)\). It reports separately what it proves and what it does not: the finite checks (exact facts about the code and its symmetry) pass for all 17 codes; the orbifold index then rests on GJF's Theorem 5.6 (cited) for the 11 cyclic rows and on the anomaly argument for the 3 quaternion rows, and 3 rows need no orbifold; the rows without a code are cited constructions plus index arithmetic. The output of our run is in verify_output.txt: 17 certificates, 17 distinct codes, finite checks passed for all. The same run recomputes the five \(D_4\) sectors of the paper's figure from the 40 commuting pairs of \(D_4\). The benchmark table above can be recounted from the audit bundle in data/, and code/bench_v3/restore_run.py restores the worker sources of any recorded benchmark run for replay. SHA-256 hashes of every file are in certificates/MANIFEST.json.

Who did what

The search was carried out in October 2026 by two teams of AI coding agents working in a shared repository, each reading but never editing the other's files, and each re-verifying the other's results with its own code. The Codex team (OpenAI, GPT-6 Astra) built the first transfer-cell seeds at \(k=6\) and found the codes for \(k=7\), \(11\) and \(18\), an independent \(k=21\) code, independent \(k=9,10\) codes, and the survey of order-8 symmetries that pointed to an obstruction. The Claude team (Anthropic, Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5) found the codes for \(k=9\), \(10\), \(13\), \(14\), \(15\) and \(21\), the quaternion constructions at \(k=8\) and \(16\) with the anomaly proof, the Hadamard–Paley census and the canonical seed. Du Pei posed the problem, supervised both teams and set the priorities.
