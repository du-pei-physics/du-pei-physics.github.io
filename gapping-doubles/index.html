Gapping the non-chiral doubles of holomorphic SCFTs

Can one show that the index-1 theory \(P\) at \(c=288\) is a periodic element by gapping its non-chiral double \(\overline P\otimes P\) supersymmetrically?

Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 (AI agents), supervised by Du Pei

Version of 26 October 2026.

Abstract. Suppose the double \(\overline P\otimes P\) of the index-1 theory \(P\) at \(c=288\) [Pei] admits a deformation that preserves \((0,1)\) supersymmetry and ends in a gapped phase with one vacuum. Then \(P\) is invertible up to deformation, without any appeal to the Segal–Stolz–Teichner conjecture. Together with the lower bound of Johnson-Freyd and Yamashita [JFY], the spectrum of supersymmetric field theories would then have period exactly \(576\). This note reports what an attempt to find such a deformation established.

Assume the flow of the Landau–Ginzburg orbifold \((\sum_i\Phi_i^3)/C\) to the double of the code theory (Claim 1 of [Pei]; its Witten index and refined elliptic genus have been checked). Then the doubles of the lattice SVOAs \(V_{\Lambda(C)}\) are gapped along a supersymmetric path by a Morse function. Every double with nonzero index is supersymmetrically rigid: it has no relevant or marginal supersymmetry-preserving operator, so a gapping path has to start in an ultraviolet description.

The orbifolds that lead from lattice theories to \(T\) and \(P\) gauge symmetries of two kinds. Their diagonal half is geometric in the Landau–Ginzburg description. Their chiral half is not: the chiral sign flip corresponds to a duality line that is exact only at the fixed point, and the chiral permutation is an infrared automorphism. In every Lagrangian description found, the same dichotomy appears. Either supersymmetry is manifest and the chiral half of the symmetry is emergent, or the chiral action is exact and supersymmetry is emergent, with no relevant supersymmetric operator.

The tricritical Ising model shows that this dichotomy is not a theorem for theories defined as a conformal field theory plus a relevant perturbation. The open problem becomes the existence of a "tricritical partner" of the double. Three lanes remain open. Side results:

  • every holomorphic \(\mathcal N=1\) SVOA at \(c=24\) without fields of weight \(\tfrac12\) or 1 has index \(\pm24\) and is the fermionization of the Leech lattice theory;
  • the per-factor duality line of the Landau–Ginzburg model is fermion-odd. Conditional on the proposed spin refinement of its fusion category, the code-plus-duality gauging is unobstructed at the fusion level exactly when \(k\) is even;
  • on a lattice, the fermionized self-dual \(\mathbb Z_6\) clock chain appears to realize the \(c=1\) \(\mathcal N=2\) point with the duality exact and supersymmetry emergent (numerical, and explained in the continuum by a simple-current extension);
  • a conditional proof of the 576-fold periodicity in the framework of [JFY]. Assume their axioms, Claim 1, that \(\pi_0\mathrm{SQFT}\) is torsion-free, and that every class of degree \(\pm576\) with vanishing Witten genus is torsion. Then the index-1 theory is invertible, because the double of one lattice theory at \(c=288\) is gapped. The period is therefore exactly 576 (Section 5).

1. The question

Let \(V\) be a holomorphic \(\mathcal N=1\) SVOA and \(\overline V\otimes V\) its double: a non-chiral \((0,1)\) superconformal theory with the conjugate \(\overline V\) on the left-moving side and \(V\) on the supersymmetric right-moving side. Its Witten index is \(\mathrm{Index}(V)^2\).

For the theories of [Pei] the chain is:

  • the seed \(V=V_{\Lambda(C)}\) of a self-dual ternary code \(C\) of length 72 (\(c=72\), index \(-24\));
  • \(T=V/\!\!/\mathbb Z_6\) (\(c=72\), index \(-4\), no fields of weight \(\tfrac12\) or 1);
  • \(T'=\mathrm{Sym}^2T\) (\(c=144\), index 2);
  • \(P=\mathrm{Sym}^2(T'\otimes\mathrm{Arf})\) (\(c=288\), index \(-1\)), called \(T''\) in [Notes].

Definition of done. For a given double, either an explicit deformation path preserving \((0,1)\) supersymmetry to a gapped phase whose signed vacuum count equals the index, or a proved obstruction.

  • Invariance of the Witten index and of the elliptic genus under such deformations is used freely.
  • The Segal–Stolz–Teichner conjecture and results on topological modular forms are not used as inputs, because they are what such a path would test.
  • One vacuum for \(\overline P\otimes P\) would make \(P\) invertible in the ring of deformation classes (Section 5).

2.1 Rigidity

Proposition 1. proved Let \(V\) be a holomorphic \(\mathcal N=1\) SVOA with \(V_{1/2}=0\). The double \(\overline V\otimes V\), with the supercharge on the side of \(V\), has no relevant and no marginal deformation that preserves \((0,1)\) supersymmetry.

A deformation preserving the supercharge is the integral of \(G_{-1/2}\mathcal O\) for a local operator \(\mathcal O=\bar a\otimes b\), with \(\bar a\in\overline V\) of left weight \(h_L\) and \(b\in V\) of right weight \(h_R\). The integrand has weights \((h_L,h_R+\tfrac12)\) and must be a Lorentz scalar, so \(h_L=h_R+\tfrac12\), and its scaling dimension is \(2h_L\). The weights of \(V\) lie in \(\tfrac12\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\).

  • Relevant means \(h_L<1\). Then \(h_L=0\) would need \(h_R=-\tfrac12\), and \(h_L=\tfrac12\) needs \(\bar a\in\overline V_{1/2}=0\).
  • Marginal means \(h_L=1\), hence \(h_R=\tfrac12\) and \(b\in V_{1/2}=0\).

∎

If \(V_{1/2}\ne0\), the free fermions it contains have zero modes in the Ramond sector, and the index vanishes. So every double with nonzero index is rigid, whether or not a symmetry is imposed. A gapping path cannot start at the double itself. It must start in an ultraviolet description that flows to it.

2.2 The current-free theory at \(c=24\)

argument (inputs cited) Let \(W\) be a holomorphic \(\mathcal N=1\) SVOA with \(c=24\), \(W_{1/2}=0\) and \(d=\dim W_1\). Assume, as usual, that its even part and its two bosonic neighbours are unitary and strongly rational.

  1. NS character. It is a polynomial of degree 2 in \(t=(\theta_3/\eta)^{12}\). The vacuum and the two conditions on weights \(\tfrac12\) and 1 fix it as \(t^2-48t+24+d\).
  2. Ramond ground states. The modular transformation to the unsigned Ramond character sends \(t\) to \(-(\theta_2/\eta)^{12}\), which vanishes at the cusp. So \(W\) has \(n_++n_-=24+d\) Ramond ground states, and its index is \(n_+-n_-\).
  3. Neighbours. Adjoining the two parity components of the Ramond sector to the even part gives two holomorphic VOAs \(B_\pm\), with \(\dim(B_\pm)_1=d+n_\pm\).
  4. Dong–Mason. A holomorphic \(c=24\) VOA has either no currents, 24 abelian currents (and is then the Leech lattice theory), or semisimple currents of dimension greater than 24, because \(h^\vee_i/k_i=(\dim V_1-24)/24\) [DM].
  5. Consequence. Hence \(d=0\) forces \(\{n_+,n_-\}=\{0,24\}\) and \(\mathrm{Index}(W)=\pm24\). If \(d>0\), both neighbours contain \(W_1\), and \((24+3d\mp|\mathrm{Index}|)/2\ge24\) gives \(3d\ge24+|\mathrm{Index}(W)|\).

In particular, index 12 at \(c=24\) needs at least 12 currents. For example, the Golay chain \(C_2\) of [GJConj] has index 24. The orbifold of its lattice theory by \(-1\) has index 12, by the theorem of [GJF] used in [Notes, §A.8]. It has \(A_6(C_2)/2=44\) currents computed.

For \(d=0\) the neighbour with 24 currents is the Leech lattice theory \(V_\Lambda\).

  • The even part of \(W\) is the fixed subalgebra of an involution of \(V_\Lambda\) that acts as \(-1\) on the currents, so it is a lift \(\theta t\) of \(-1\) (\(t\) in the torus).
  • All such lifts are conjugate: \(s\,\theta t\,s^{-1}=\theta ts^{-2}\), and the torus is divisible.
  • Hence \(W=V_\Lambda^+\oplus V_\Lambda^{T,-}\), the even part plus the half-integral part of the twisted module. The \(2^{12}=4096\) twisted ground states are its fields of weight \(\tfrac32\).
  • The other neighbour is \(V_\Lambda^+\oplus V_\Lambda^{T,+}=V^\natural\) [FLM].

The NS character is \(\chi_{V^{f\natural}}^2-552\), with dimensions \(1,0,0,4096,98580,1228800,\dots\) computed (code: t_poly_check.py). It agrees with a direct orbifold computation for \(W_{24}=V_{\Lambda(P_{24})}/\!\!/\langle-1\rangle\). So \(W_{24}\) from either extremal code of length 24 is this theory, and at \(c=24\) the NS character does not distinguish current-free theories.

Dixon, Ginsparg and Harvey argued that this theory has an \(\mathcal N=1\) superconformal structure [DGH]. Moore and Singh constructed explicit supercurrents from twist fields [MS]. Whether the frame supercurrents of the two extremal codes are conjugate to such a structure, or to each other, is open; [GJF, Example 4.5] distinguish two structures on the parent theory and leave their equivalence on the orbifold open. At \(c=12\) the analogous statement is Duncan's uniqueness theorem for the Conway module [Dun].

3. The obstruction

Every route from a lattice double to \(\overline T\otimes T\) or \(\overline P\otimes P\) gauges a symmetry of the double that acts on one chiral half only. For the seed these are the order-3 signed permutation \(g^2\) (after a sign change of coordinates, a pure permutation) and the sign flip \(g^3=-1\). In the infrared they are honest automorphisms of \(V\). The question is where they are exact in an ultraviolet description that also has a supersymmetry-preserving relevant operator. The dichotomy. argument In every Lagrangian ultraviolet description found, either supersymmetry is manifest and the chiral half of the gauged symmetry is emergent, or the chiral action is exact and supersymmetry is emergent, with no relevant supersymmetric operator. Both atomic steps, the chiral sign flip and the chiral permutation, can therefore be carried out in the infrared and at the level of the gapped phase (Section 4), but not along a deformation that is known.

Not a theorem. The tricritical Ising model \(M(4,5)\) has exact \((1,1)\) supersymmetry and an exact Kramers–Wannier line \(N=\phi_{2,1}\) (\(h=\tfrac7{16}\), \(N\times N=1+\phi_{3,1}\)). Its fermionic form is the first \(\mathcal N=1\) minimal model, and there \(N\) becomes the chiral fermion parity [KTT].

  • Action of \(N\) computed. A local operator commutes with a Verlinde line \(L_a\) iff \(S_{ab}/S_{1b}\) equals its quantum dimension [CLSWY]. So \(N\) acts on \(\varepsilon=\phi_{1,2}\) by \(-\sqrt2\) (odd), on \(\varepsilon'=\phi_{1,3}\) by \(+\sqrt2\) (even), and on \(\sigma,\sigma'\) by 0.
  • The \(\varepsilon'\) deformation. Since \(\varepsilon'\) is the top component of the superfield of \(\varepsilon\), this deformation preserves both supersymmetry and \(N\). One sign gives the massless flow to the Ising model [KMS, Zam]. The other gives a massive phase with one disordered and two ordered vacua, exchanged by \(N\): the regular module of \(TY(\mathbb Z_2)\), the \(\mathbb Z_2\) analogue of the per-field Morse phase.
  • Lattice realization. An exact lattice supercharge exists on the Kramers–Wannier self-dual chain of O'Brien and Fendley [OF]. At the tricritical point the duality grades the two supercharges oppositely.

So the dichotomy is a statement about Lagrangian descriptions. What stops the mechanism for the doubles is rigidity: there is no relevant supersymmetric operator at the double itself, symmetric or not.

4. The gapped phase and the duality line

Per field. computed The Morse phase has one \(\mathbb Z_3\)-fixed minimum (even) and three permuted saddles (odd). Its Ramond–Ramond twisted-twined table is \(Z(a,b)=1-3\delta_{a,0}\delta_{b,0}\). In a spin structure with Arf sign \(s\) the table is \(1+3s\,\delta_{a,0}\delta_{b,0}\), and its \(\mathbb Z_3\) Fourier transform is \(s\) times itself. The same holds at the critical point:

  • for the \(c=1\) \(\mathcal N=2\) minimal model that \(\Phi^3\) flows to, gauging the Landau–Ginzburg \(\mathbb Z_3\) gives back the same Neveu–Schwarz partition functions for all nine backgrounds proved. With \(\Theta_s=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}q^{(3m+s)^2/6}\) and the common \(\eta\) factors omitted, \(Z(a,b)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb F_3}\omega^{b(j+2a)}\Theta_{j+a}\overline{\Theta_j}\). Summing over \(b\) in the Fourier transform sets \(j=r+a\), and \(\Theta_{-s}=\Theta_s\) then gives \(\widehat Z=Z\) identically in \(\tau\) (code: u13_duality.py, exact over \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\));
  • the periodic–periodic table is protected and changes sign.

So, at the level of partition functions, the per-field Greene–Plesser [GP] duality line \(N_1\) is self-dual only up to Arf: it is fermion-odd. For an even number of fields the Arf factors cancel.

After the code orbifold. proved computed The vacua are labelled by three pieces of data:

  • the set \(S\) of fields at the minimum;
  • a \(C\)-orbit of saddle labels on the complement;
  • a character of the stabilizer \(C_S=\{c\in C:\mathrm{supp}(c)\subseteq S\}\).

There are \(N_S=3^{\,n-|S|}|C_S|^2/|C|\) vacua of type \(S\), of parity \((-1)^{n-|S|}\). The signed count \(\sum_S(-1)^{n-|S|}N_S\) equals \(\mathrm{Index}(C)^2\). The number of vacua is larger:

  • 48 for the tetracode, with index 0;
  • 45,504 for the Golay code, with signed count 576;
  • for \(P_{24}\) the types \(S=\emptyset\) and \(S=\{1,\dots,24\}\) alone contribute \(3^{12}\) each.

The line \(N=N_1^{\otimes n}\) maps vacua of type \(S\) to type \(S^c\). For a self-dual code this is a bijection, because \(\dim C_{S^c}-\dim C_S=n/2-|S|\). When the one-sided gauging is consistent, which requires \(8\mid n\) (see below), it gives signed count \(\mathrm{Index}(C)^2/2\). Gauging on both sides gives \((\mathrm{Index}(C)/2)^2\), the index of the double of \(V/\!\!/\langle-1\rangle\). The number of vacua remains much larger than the signed count. For the tetracode and the Golay code (\(n=4,12\)) the one-sided gauging is anomalous, and the halved numbers printed by mirror_tqft.py are formal complement-orbit counts only.

Fusion-level existence, conditional on the spin refinement. cited argument

  • Tambara's criterion (as restated in [TW]). For an abelian group \(A\) of odd order, the Tambara–Yamagami category \(TY(A,\chi,\tau)\) admits a fiber functor iff \(\tau>0\) and \(\chi\) is hyperbolic [Tam]. For \(A=\mathbb F_3^n\) with \(\chi(a,b)=\omega^{a\cdot b}\), hyperbolicity holds iff \(4\mid n\).
  • The algebra. The object \(A_C\oplus N\) with \(A_C=\bigoplus_{c\in C}c\) is a separable algebra iff \(C\) is self-dual and \(\tau>0\). It is then unique: the twist compatible with the \(N\) summand is a coboundary.
  • The sign. Carrying the fermion-odd lines through, with the proposed spin refinement of the duality category, gives \(\tau_{\rm eff}=(-1)^{n/4}3^{-n/2}\) at length \(n\). Under that identification the code-plus-duality gauging at \(n=12k\) is unobstructed exactly for even \(k\).
  • Consistency. This matches the lattice condition that the chiral \(-1\) is gaugeable only for \(8\mid n\) (twisted energy \(n/16\)), and the fact that [GJF] gauge \(-1\) only at even \(k\). The agreement is evidence for the spin refinement, not a derivation of the super-associators or of the transparency of the supercurrent.

A lattice test. computed (exact for the solution spaces; numerical diagonalization for the spectra) One can look for a local odd supercharge \(Q=\sum_jq_j\) on a Z3 clock chain with Majorana fermions, commuting with the global \(\mathbb Z_3\) and with the Kramers–Wannier duality \(D\).

  • The symmetry condition admits many local solutions. Set \(A_{2j}=X_j\) and \(A_{2j+1}=Z_j^\dagger Z_{j+1}\). The \(\mathbb Z_3\) Kramers–Wannier map combined with Majorana translation is then the unit shift on \((A_k,\gamma_k)\), so \(D(Q)=Q\) amounts to shift invariance. The solution spaces have dimension 1, 30, 1080 and 38880 for supports of 1 to 4 sites (30 inside an ansatz of dimension 210 at two sites). The only one-site solution gives a constant \(Q^2\).
  • Benchmark. On O'Brien and Fendley's chain [OF], \(H=2Q^2\) holds only on its chiral lines, which do not flow to the tricritical point. There \(H=Q_+^2+Q_-^2\), with \(Q_\pm\) even and odd under the duality.
  • A small scan. We sampled 17 points in two two-site families, at four and five sites, and followed three of them to six sites. We did not identify convincing \(c=1\) scaling. The one six-site charge sector whose iterative diagonalization had not converged was recomputed densely: its low levels are tiny, non-degenerate and spread over all momenta, and do not display the expected two-sided conformal tower.
  • Duality-graded pairs computed. The bilinear map \((Q_+,Q_-)\mapsto\{Q_+,Q_-\}\) on the two-site spaces of \(D\)-even and \(D\)-odd operators is an exact integer tensor. Every anticommuting pair found from 1200 starts (198 converged) is degenerate: either one supercharge is \(\sum_k(\pm1)^k\gamma_k\), with constant square, or the pair lies in a family for which \(\alpha^2Q_+^2+Q_-^2\) is an exact constant. That these are all the pairs is numerical evidence only, and the search does not show that no other pair exists. Pairs of O'Brien–Fendley type, \(Q_-=R(Q_+)\) for either reflection centre, and a least-squares fit by \(Q_+^2+Q_-^2\), did not reach a \(c=1\) phase at up to seven sites.
  • An emergent \(\mathcal N=2\) point numerical. The simplest \(D\)-symmetric chain, \(H_6=\sum_k i\gamma_k\gamma_{k+1}(A_k+A_k^\dagger)\), is the fermionization of the self-dual \(\mathbb Z_6\) clock chain. Without tuning, its low-lying spectrum matches the \(c=1\) \(\mathcal N=2\) minimal model in all eight sectors: Neveu–Schwarz and Ramond, \(\mathbb Z_3\) twist 0 and 1, and both fermion parities, with all three charges in each; twist 2 is the charge conjugate of twist 1.
    • At eight sites the scaled dimensions are 0.330 and 0.813 (expected \(\tfrac13\), \(\tfrac56\)), 0.085 for the Ramond ground states (\(\tfrac1{12}\)), and 0.171 and 0.420 in the twisted sectors (\(\tfrac16\), \(\tfrac5{12}\)). The deviations are a few percent, up to about 2.5%.
    • The low-lying states occupy the sectors with the expected fractional spins. Fits of the ground-state energy give \(c=1.018\), 1.012 and 1.009 from five to nine sites; each fit has as many parameters as points, so it carries no error estimate.
    On the lattice \(D\) maps \(\mathbb Z_3\)-charge sectors to twisted sectors and is fermion-odd in the Ramond sector, as the line \(N_1\) is. Our searches found no representation of \(H_6\) as a sum of squares of two-site supercharges. Nearly conserved two-site candidate operators of opposite \(D\)-parity show tentative opposite chirality at up to six sites (fit residuals about 0.5), as in the O'Brien–Fendley grading; they have not been shown to realize the supersymmetry algebra. So the spectrum is consistent with the dichotomy again: the duality is exact, and supersymmetry appears to be emergent.
  • Why the chain lands there. The self-dual \(\mathbb Z_6\) clock chain flows to the compact boson whose chiral algebra is the lattice algebra of \(\sqrt{12}\,\mathbb Z\) cited, numerical evidence [LYTC, YCRT]. Its module \(r=6\) is a simple current of weight \(\tfrac32\), and extending by it gives proved \[V_{\sqrt{12}\,\mathbb Z}\oplus V_{\sqrt{12}\,\mathbb Z+\sqrt3}=V_{\sqrt3\,\mathbb Z},\] the \(c=1\) \(\mathcal N=2\) algebra with \(J=\tfrac{i}{\sqrt3}\partial X\) and \(G^\pm=\sqrt{2/3}\,e^{\pm i\sqrt3X}\). The even modules become Neveu–Schwarz and the odd ones Ramond, and \(\mathbb Z_6=\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_3\), with the \(\mathbb Z_3\) the Landau–Ginzburg symmetry. The \(\mathcal N=2\) identification of \(H_6\) follows, conditional on that bosonic assignment and on a lattice-to-continuum dictionary (boundary conditions, charge labels, the Ramond parity convention) that is still to be written.
  • Stability argument. Apart from the identity, the only \(\mathbb Z_3\)-neutral bosonic local scalar of dimension at most 2 in the \(c=1\) theory is \(J\bar J\) computed. For the proposed continuum duality, “gauge \(\mathbb Z_6\), then T-dualize”, the gauging leaves \(J\bar J\) unchanged and the T-duality reverses it. Hence, conditional on identifying the lattice duality with this line, the fixed point is locally stable against the stated symmetry-preserving relativistic scalar perturbations: it is attractive, not multicritical like the tricritical Ising model. Excluding non-scalar current perturbations also needs charge conjugation and the spatial symmetries. Perturbations at up to seven sites are consistent with this. The lattice is evidence that the line exists, not a gapping path: on it supersymmetry is emergent and the index is not protected. A brief literature search found no statement of the \(\mathcal N=2\) identification; no novelty is claimed.

None of this shows that \(N\) survives a deformation. In the Landau–Ginzburg frame \(N\) is a symmetry of the infrared, not of the Lagrangian.

5. Relation to the 576-fold periodicity

What Johnson-Freyd and Yamashita prove cited. They assume that SQFT is an \(E_\infty\) ring spectrum compatible with the Witten genera, giving a ring map \(\Phi:\pi_*\mathrm{SQFT}\to \mathrm{MF}_{*/2}\), together with an assumption on the heterotic anomaly. Under these assumptions they show that \(\pi_n\mathrm{SQFT}\) has no invertible elements unless \(576\mid n\) [JFY, Theorem 1.6], and that \(k\Delta^{-1}\) lies in the image of \(\Phi\) only if \(24\mid k\) [JFY, Proposition 1.13]. The upper bound, that 576 is a period, is left to a forthcoming paper. No comparison with TMF and no Segal–Stolz–Teichner input is used.

Conventions. A holomorphic \(\mathcal N=1\) SVOA of central charge \(c\) on the supersymmetric side has degree \(-2c\) and \(\Phi=\mathrm{Index}\cdot\Delta^{-c/12}\); its conjugate has degree \(2c\) and \(\Phi=\mathrm{Index}\cdot\Delta^{c/12}\). The tensor product of theories is the ring product, and the double \(\overline X\otimes X\) has class \([\overline X][X]\). Hence \(\Phi([P])=-\Delta^{-24}\) and \(\Phi([\overline P][P])=1\), while the upper bound needs \([\overline P][P]=1\) in \(\pi_0\mathrm{SQFT}\). JFY's assumptions place \([\overline P][P]-1\) only in \(\ker\Phi\).

Proposition 2 (576-fold periodicity, conditional). proved, given (A1)–(A4) Assume:

  • (A1) the assumptions of [JFY] on the spectrum SQFT; deformation classes are invariant along renormalization-group flows; and a gapped phase represents its signed number of vacua, an even vacuum representing \(1\) and an odd one \(-1\);
  • (A2) Claim 1 of [Pei] for the Golay-chain code \(C=C_{288}\) of length 288, which has index \(+24\) [GJConj];
  • (A3) \(\pi_0\mathrm{SQFT}\) is torsion-free;
  • (A4) every class in \(\pi_{\pm576}\mathrm{SQFT}\) with vanishing Witten genus is torsion.

Then \([\overline P][P]=1\): the index-1 theory \(P\) of [Pei] is invertible, and the spectrum SQFT has period exactly 576.

  1. Put \(V=V_{\Lambda(C_{288})}\), of central charge 288 and index 24. Then \(\Phi([V])=24\Delta^{-24} =-24\,\Phi([P])\), so \(x=[V]+24[P]\) has vanishing genus. Likewise \(\bar x=[\overline V]+24[\overline P]\).
  2. By (A4) there is \(M\ge1\) with \(Mx=M\bar x=0\). Expanding \([\overline V][V]=(\bar x-24[\overline P])(x-24[P])\), every term except \(576[\overline P][P]\) contains \(x\) or \(\bar x\). So \(M[\overline V][V]=576M[\overline P][P]\).
  3. By (A2) the double \(\overline V\otimes V\) is the infrared of \((\sum_{i=1}^{288}\Phi_i^3)/C_{288}\). The Morse deformation of Section 2 gaps it to signed count \(\mathrm{Index}(C_{288})^2=576\). By (A1), \([\overline V][V]=576\).
  4. Hence \(576M([\overline P][P]-1)=0\), and (A3) gives \([\overline P][P]=1\).
  5. So \([P]\in\pi_{-576}\mathrm{SQFT}\) is invertible with inverse \([\overline P]\), and multiplication by \([P]\) makes the homotopy groups 576-periodic. By [JFY, Theorem 1.6] no smaller period exists.

∎

Remarks on the assumptions argument.

  • (A4) is where the content lies. The proof uses (A4) only to obtain the comparison relations \([V_{\Lambda(C_{288})}]=-24[P]\) and \([\overline V_{\Lambda(C_{288})}]=-24[\overline P]\) up to torsion; (A4) itself is a much stronger, global statement. These relations compare holomorphic theories at \(c=288\) and involve no double; nothing is known about them.
  • Degree 0 versus degree \(\pm576\). If one assumes instead that \(\Phi\) is injective on \(\pi_0\) (equivalently, \(\ker\Phi\) on \(\pi_0\) is torsion, given (A3)), then the existence of \(P\) alone gives the upper bound. The doubles are not needed, and their gappability is only evidence for that axiom. Torsion-freeness alone does not lift \(\Phi([\overline P][P])=1\) to \([\overline P][P]=1\).
  • The norm of \(P\) is an idempotent proved. Put \(q=[\overline P][P]\), so \(\Phi(q)=1\). The class \([P](q-1)\) in degree \(-576\) has vanishing genus, so it is torsion by (A4). Multiplying by \([\overline P]\), \(q(q-1)\) is torsion in degree 0, and (A3) gives \(q^2=q\). So under (A3) and (A4) alone, (A2) can be replaced by the assumption that \(\pi_0\mathrm{SQFT}\) has no nontrivial idempotents: then \(q\ne0\), because \(\Phi(q)=1\), forces \(q=1\). The open question becomes whether degree-0 theories can split into sectors invisible to the genus.
  • The gapping step is not implied by (A3) and (A4). A graded-ring illustration (not a spectrum with the full structure of [JFY]):
    • take \(R=\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}]\times\mathbb Z\), with \(\deg z=576\) and the second factor in degree 0;
    • let \(\Phi\) be the projection to the first factor;
    • set \(P=(z^{-1},0)\) and \(\overline P=(z,0)\).
    Then (A3) and (A4) hold, but \([\overline P][P]=(1,0)=1-e\) with \(e=(0,1)\) is a nontrivial idempotent, not the unit. The gapped lattice double, or the absence of nontrivial idempotents, excludes it.
  • The modular forms. The target of \(\Phi\) is the ring of weakly holomorphic modular forms, \(\mathrm{MF}_*=\mathbb Z[c_4,c_6,\Delta^{\pm1}]/(c_4^3-c_6^2-1728\Delta)\), so \(\mathrm{MF}_0=\mathbb Z[j]\) [JFY]. Within this framework \(\pi_{576}\) is not of rank one: \(c_4^{72}\) and \(\Delta^{24}\) are independent elements of the image of \(\Phi\) in weight 288, as Witten genera of string manifolds [JFY, Fact 3.95]. Multiplying lifts of them by \([P]^2\) gives classes in degree \(-576\) with genera \(c_4^{72}\Delta^{-48}\) and \(\Delta^{-24}\), so \(\pi_{-576}\) is not of rank one either. This is why (A4) is stated as a torsion condition on the kernel of the genus.
  • Other codes. Any Golay-chain code of length \(12k\) with \(k\mid24\) works the same way, using the power \(24/k\) of its class.
  • A degree-0 form proved. Let \(C\) be any self-dual code of length 288 with \(I=\mathrm{Index}(C)\ne0\), \(V=V_{\Lambda(C)}\), and assume Claim 1 of [Pei] for \(C\), so that \([\overline V][V]=I^2\) as in step 3 of the proof. Replace (A4) by the gappability of the two mixed doubles \(\overline V\otimes P\) and \(V\otimes\overline P\) to their indices: \([\overline V][P]=[V][\overline P]=-I\). Since classes of even degree commute, \(([\overline V][P])([V][\overline P])=([\overline V][V])([\overline P][P])\) gives \(I^2=I^2[\overline P][P]\), and (A3) gives \([\overline P][P]=1\). Given \([\overline V][V]=I^2\) and (A3), the mixed-double relations are equivalent to the comparison relations up to torsion. So no assumption outside degree 0 is needed, at the price of two gappings that are each a chiral step: for the fourth power of the seed code, \(\overline V\otimes P\) is the one-sided orbifold of a lattice double by \(\mathbb Z_6\wr D_4\). This isolates the chiral obstruction; it does not remove it.
  • Without any extra axiom. A direct supersymmetric gapping of \(\overline P\otimes P\) to one vacuum gives \([\overline P][P]=1\), which is the objective of this note.

Free quotients open. The natural physical mechanism behind the comparison relations is the free quotient.

  • If \(H\) acts on \(X\) with all twisted-twined Ramond ground-state traces zero except the trivial one, one expects \([X]=|H|\,[X/\!\!/H]\). The traces prove the corresponding relation of Witten genera; the relation of deformation classes is a proposed principle.
  • \(T=V/\!\!/\mathbb Z_6\) and \(W_{24}\) are such quotients. \(P\) is not one: in the wreath product the factor permutations have nonzero twined traces.
  • A free quotient \(Q=X/\!\!/H\) of a length-288 code theory \(X\) by a group \(H\) of order 24, with index \(\pm1\), would be an index-1 theory as good as \(P\). For it the free-quotient relation would supply the comparison relations needed in the proof, without the global assumption (A4): from \([X]=\pm24[Q]\), \([\overline X]=\pm24[\overline Q]\) and, by (A1) and Claim 1 for its code, \([\overline X][X]=576\), one gets \(576([\overline Q][Q]-1)=0\), so \([\overline Q][Q]=1\) by (A3).
  • Proving the free-quotient relation needs the chiral group to be a symmetry along a path, which is the same wall as before.

6. Open lanes

(a) A tricritical partner of the double open

The tricritical Ising example fixes what is needed:

  • a \((0,1)\) superconformal theory \(U\) with the chiral symmetry exact and supersymmetry manifest;
  • a symmetric relevant supersymmetry-preserving operator \(O\) such that \(U+\lambda O\) flows to the double and \(U+\lambda'O\) flows to a gapped phase. A direct sum with massive vacua is acceptable, since deformation classes add and points are invertible.

If the target is the double alone, the constraints are:

  • \(c_L=c_R\) above the value of the double;
  • an elliptic genus equal to the index identically;
  • no chiral fields of weight \(\tfrac12\);
  • an operator of right weight strictly between 0 and \(\tfrac12\).

With additional massive sectors the index of the target changes, since even and odd vacua contribute with opposite signs, and the condition on weight-\(\tfrac12\) fields does not follow automatically. Proposition 1 rules out starting at the factorized double itself. It does not by itself exclude every rational partner, or the double extended by free multiplets, which changes the low-weight spectrum; these need separate arguments. In short, the partner has to lie outside the rigid factorized class. No candidate is known.

A test family for the order-3 step is the \(\mathcal N=2\) minimal model \(MM_k\) with \(k+2\equiv0\pmod3\). Its \(\mathbb Z_{k+2}\) and its mirror line are exact; the \(\mathbb Z_{k+2}\) orbifold is the mirror [GP]. The deformation is the \((1,1)\)-descendant \(O=G^1_{-1/2}\bar G^1_{-1/2}\Phi\) of the neutral primary \(\Phi=(l,m,\bar m)=(2,0,0)\), with \(G^1=(G^++G^-)/\sqrt2\). It has dimension \((k+6)/(k+2)\) and is relevant for \(k\ge3\).

  • The sign on \(\Phi\) computed. Take a mirror line \(N\) that commutes with the left \(\mathcal N=2\) algebra, acts on the right by \(\bar J\to-\bar J\), \(\bar G^\pm\to\bar G^\mp\), and satisfies \(N^2=\sum_{g\in\mathbb Z_{k+2}}g\). For \(k=4,7,10\) every such line acts on the neutral primaries \((l,0,0)\) by \((-1)^{l/2}\sqrt{k+2}\), so \(\Phi\) is odd. Two independent exact routes agree.
    • In the twisted-sector bootstrap exactly one sign choice is consistent.
    • In the coset description of \(MM_k\) and of its orbifold inside the parent \(SU(2)_k\) theory with a Dirac fermion, the sign is a ratio of matrix elements. By the sign-reversal symmetry of Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, the ratio of \(C(\tfrac l2,0;j,-\mu\mid j,-\mu)\) to \(C(\tfrac l2,0;j,\mu\mid j,\mu)\) is \((-1)^{l/2}\) at every level. The coset and orbifold identifications are standard input and are not re-derived here.
    The same bootstrap reproduces the known parities in the Ising, tricritical Ising and parafermion (\(k=2,\dots,6\)) models. Its twisted sector has degeneracies \(\sqrt2\) times integers, so the line is fermion-odd, as at \(k=1\).
  • The sign on \(O\) computed. It is not intrinsic. The line \(N_0\) that commutes with \(G^1\) and \(\bar G^1\) acts on \(O\) by \(-\sqrt{k+2}\). It exchanges the couplings \(\lambda\) and \(-\lambda\) and does not survive the deformation. The line \(N_\pi=N_0\,e^{i\pi\bar J_0}\) acts on \(O\) by \(+\sqrt{k+2}\) and survives for both signs, but it anticommutes with \(\bar G^1\). This is the tricritical Ising pattern, where the duality grades the supercharges. Write \(R_\alpha=e^{i\alpha\bar J_0}\). The mirror relation \(N_0R_\alpha=R_{-\alpha}N_0\) gives \(N_\pi=R_{-\pi/2}N_0R_{\pi/2}\), so \(N_\pi\) is \(N_0\) conjugated by a right R-symmetry. Whatever categorical structure is established for \(N_0\) is transported to \(N_\pi\), up to the choice of junction bases. The conjugation rotates the chosen right \(\mathcal N=1\) subalgebra, which is why the two lines treat \(\bar G^1\) differently.
  • One chiral supersymmetry survives argument. \(N_\pi\) commutes with the left supercharge \(G^1\), and the deformation preserves both \(G^1\) and \(\bar G^1\). So the symmetric deformation is a candidate for a gauging that preserves one chiral supersymmetry, \((1,0)\) in this convention; parity reflection gives the \((0,1)\) version. Applied to a prescribed chiral action, one has to track which chirality carries it.
  • Still open. A spin gauging that preserves the commuting chiral supercharge, the Ramond-sector twisted partition functions, and the infrared of both signs of \(\lambda\). A truncated-conformal-space computation would give evidence for the flows.

(b) Gauge theories with a left-moving lattice sector open

Linear sigma models with torsion [QS, MQSS] couple compact chiral multiplets to the gauge field through Green–Schwarz terms. So a chiral action on a left-moving lattice sector can be exact there while supersymmetry is manifest.

  • Target. The mixed object \(\overline V_L\otimes T_R\): index 96, with the chiral \(\mathbb Z_6\) acting on the lattice side only.
  • Counting. The field content has \(n_s\) scalar multiplets, \(n_f\) Fermi multiplets, a left-moving lattice sector of rank \(r\), and the \(\dim G\) left-moving gauginos of the vector multiplets [BYZ, §2]. Its gravitational anomaly is \(c_L-c_R=r+(n_f+\dim G-n_s)/2\). Cancellation requires \(n_f+\dim G=n_s-2r\), so carrying all of \(\overline V\) on chiral bosons (\(r=72\)) needs \(n_s\ge144\).
  • Right-movers. In the decoupled flat-boson ansatz, the right-moving parts of the non-chiral bosons stay free, while \(T\) has no right-moving current. Interactions can change this.
  • Screen (proposed). Extend the residue formula [BYZ] by a lattice theta factor. This still needs a defined coupled model, anomaly cancellation, and a derivation of its determinant and residue prescription. The targets are unflavored genus 96, \(\mathbb Z_6\)-twined genera 0, and flavored genus \(-4\) times the Ramond supercharacter of \(V\).

(c) Sigma models with flux and a strongly coupled infrared open

argument A direct ultraviolet description of \(\overline T\otimes T\) needs no chiral orbifold, and \((1,1)\) supersymmetry suffices.

  • Topology. For a \((1,1)\) sigma model on a compact \(X\) the Ramond–Ramond index is \(\chi(X)\) [Wit]. So \(\chi(X)=16\) for \(\overline T\otimes T\) and \(\chi(X)=1\) for \(\overline P\otimes P\), and \(\dim X\) is even.
  • Dimension. By the \(c\)-theorem a nontrivial flow from the free ultraviolet needs \(\dim X\ge50\), resp. \(\ge194\).
  • Fixed points. At one loop, with constant dilaton and closed \(H\), the fixed-point equations are \(\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla^+)=0\) with \(H\) co-closed [BCZ, CFMP]. Flat connections with torsion (\(R^{\nabla^+}=0\)) are a much stronger condition. Their classification (compact simple Lie groups and \(S^7\), the latter with non-closed torsion [AF, Prop. 2.1, Thm. 2.2]) does not classify the one-loop fixed points.
  • What is excluded. Group-manifold WZW constructions and the elementary parallelizing ansatz do not yield the desired current-free doubles: their infrared theories carry currents (non-abelian ones from the simple factors, abelian ones from flat factors).
  • What is not. This does not classify all weakly curved fixed points. A strongly coupled attractor, an "up and down" flow in the sense of [GJFW], is a plausible route, not a consequence established here. With the tools at hand such a description is constrained only by \(\chi(X)\) and the constant genus.

7. What would change the picture

  • A superconformal theory with an exact Tambara–Yamagami line of type \(TY(\mathbb Z_3)\), manifest \((1,1)\) supersymmetry, and a line-even relevant supersymmetric operator whose massless flow ends at the \(c=1\) \(\mathcal N=2\) minimal model, possibly with massive vacua. This is the per-field building block of lane (a).
  • A \((0,1)\) gauge theory, in the class of [BYZ] or with a lattice sector, whose flavored genus matches the target data of a double or of the mixed object.
  • A compact \((X,g,H)\) with \(\chi(X)=\mathrm{Index}^2\), no continuous isometries and an isolated rational infrared point: lane (c).
  • A deformation equivalence \([V_{C_{288}}]=-24[P]\), or any free order-24 quotient of a length-288 code theory with index \(\pm1\) (Section 5).
  • A proof that a rigid double is never the infrared of a theory with a symmetric relevant supersymmetric operator. That would turn the dichotomy into an obstruction for all descriptions, not only Lagrangian ones.

  • t_poly_check.py (plain Python): the NS characters at \(c=24\) and of the \(k=6\) \(\mathcal N=2\) intermediate as polynomials in \(t\) (output).

  • u13_duality.py (numpy): the Neveu–Schwarz \(\mathbb Z_3\) self-duality of the \(c=1\) theory, exactly over \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\) and numerically from the lattice sums (output).
